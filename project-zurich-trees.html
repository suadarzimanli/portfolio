Zürich Trees (municipal SR research)

A design study and city-wide analysis: every resident report is anchored to one tree in the City of Zürich open Baumkataster and stays one service request from intake to closure — with public tracking that never shows who reported it. Separate from the Arborist practice inventory app.

Overview

What this project does

Residents tap the tree on the map and report the issue in four steps (tree, issue, contact, review), then follow the case on a public tracking page with their reference number. Staff work each case through inspection and work order on a desk with a queue overview. One four-phase status — received, inspection, work order, closed — is shared by map pins, desk and tracking page, and contact details are returned to signed-in staff only.

Research questions

What I set out to answer

  • How can tree-related service requests be organised so that intake, inspection, work order and closure form one traceable case for residents and staff?
  • How much location ambiguity does tree-anchored map intake remove compared with street-only and address-only descriptions — and how robust is it to imprecise touch input?
  • Which privacy requirements follow from combining public open-data layers with resident case data, and how can a system enforce them?

Key findings

What the study showed

  • Addresses rarely identify a tree: for street trees a building address leaves a median of 3 candidate trees and identifies one tree in only 14% of cases; 15% of all trees are more than 60 m from any address.
  • Among four map-selection rules tested under simulated touch error, a distance-ordered list of all trees within 10 m kept the intended tree available in 99.8% of street-tree taps even with an 8 m miss and never attached a report to the wrong tree; automatic nearest-tree selection did so in 26% of taps at a 5 m miss.
  • Public status and private identity can coexist when redaction is enforced at the API boundary rather than in the interface; the rules are covered by automated tests.
